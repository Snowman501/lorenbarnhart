Chapter 1: The Yellow Bungalow

The air inside the dark Ford SUV reeked of salt, heavy coastal humidity, and the sharp, metallic tang of copper. Mark Chen kept his left hand glued to the steering wheel, his knuckles white against the leather. His right hand rested on the heavy rubber grip of his tactical torch. Through the rain-streaked windshield, the modest, yellow-shingled bungalow on Alma Street looked completely abandoned, a rot-hollowed tooth jutting out against the San Pedro harbor. The coastal mist wrapped around the sagging roofline like suffocating fingers.

Beside him, Sarah Vance didn’t breathe. She didn’t need to anymore. Her skin, once smooth and warm, now possessed the polished, seamless luster of a silver-bronze alloy. Underneath the heavy tactical jacket covering her chest, the rhythmic, mechanical hum of her core pulsed—a low, rhythmic thrumming sound like a clockwork engine drowning in heavy oil.

“It’s localized right above the porch,” Sarah said. Her voice carried a synthesized, double-layered edge that vibrated directly against the vehicle’s steel floorboards, rattling the loose coins in the cup holder. “The magnetic pull is spiking. My visual field is throwing static every time I look at the roofline.”

Mark turned to look at her. Even after everything they had survived—from the cursed Maryland woods to the crushing damp of the Bell Witch’s cavern—seeing her like this still made his chest tighten with a cold, hollow ache. Her eyes glowed with a faint, iridescent violet hue. She wasn’t just investigating Silas Clobery’s Relic Web anymore; she was rapidly becoming part of its machinery.

“Are you sure you can handle this one?” Mark asked, his voice dropping an octave. “The San Pedro case isn’t just a lingering emotional imprint, Sarah. The historical logs call it an aggressive poltergeist. The real family fled hundreds of miles inland to Weldon, and the damn thing still followed them. But Clobery’s ledger says otherwise.”

“Clobery bound a maritime executioner here in 1698,” Sarah replied. She raised her right hand, her silver fingers catching the dim green backlighting of the dashboard. She slowly flexed them. The joint mechanisms clicked in a precise, micro-hydraulic sequence. “He used a naval navigational compass forged from high-purity copper and dark iron. It’s serving as a permanent receiver. The house isn’t just haunted, Mark. It’s a closed circuit.”

Suddenly, the vehicle’s dashboard electronics flickered wildly. The digital clock spun backward erratically, the numbers blurring into a meaningless red haze. The radio erupted into a wall of blaring, high-frequency static that sounded shockingly like a choir of men screaming underwater.

SNAP.

Every streetlight down Alma Street blew out simultaneously in a cascading shower of orange sparks.
